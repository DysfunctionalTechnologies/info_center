GPIO 21 is a good choice on a 3B+. It uses PCM, so analog headphone audio can still work. I2S HATs will not.1. Parts and power (read this before wiring)RequiredRaspberry Pi 3B+ and official 5.1 V / 2.5 A micro-USB supply for the Pi only
Newly imaged microSD (16 GB minimum, 32 GB better, A1/A2)
DS3231 module that includes the AT24C32 EEPROM
DS18B20 (TO-92 or waterproof probe)
4.7 kΩ pull-up for the DS18B20 data line
WS2812B strip / matrix
330 Ω resistor on the LED data line
1000 µF (or larger) electrolytic capacitor across LED 5 V and GND, close to the strip
Separate 5 V supply for the LEDs
Common ground between LED PSU, strip GND, and Pi GND
Optional but recommended: 3.3 V → 5 V level shifter on the LED data line

Power warning
535 WS2812B LEDs at full white can draw on the order of 30 A. Do not power the strip from the Pi 5 V pins. Size the LED supply for worst case, inject 5 V at both ends (and every ~1–2 m on a long strip), and start software brightness low. INFO_CENTER defaults around brightness 31–32, which is safer, but a wiring mistake at first boot can still brown out the Pi.Work with power disconnected while changing wiring.2. 6.2 Make the DS3231 the system clockbash

-w writes system time → RTC. -r reads RTC.If you are offline and the RTC already has a good time:bash

RPi Expansion Board Wiring:

########################################################################
# So, according to the RPi documentation and my program, where are the #
# various pins of devices connected to the RPi3B+?                     #
#                                                                      #
# - First, DS3231 - +3.3V, GND, SDA, and SCLK?                         #
# - Then DS18B20 - +3.3V, GND, and DATA?                               #
# - Finally - WS2812b LED strip - +5V, GND, and DIN (this we know to   #
#   be GPIO 21, pin 40 on the expansion connector)  - GND would be pin #
#   39, and +5V would be pins 2 & 4. And yes, I know not to try to     #
#   power the 16x32 panel from the RPi3B+                              #
########################################################################

On a Raspberry Pi 3B+ 40-pin header, this is the mapping that matches
both the official pinout and INFO_CENTER.

Physical pin = the number stamped on the header.
BCM GPIO = the number the software uses.

DS3231 (I2C-1)
DS3231       Physical pin  BCM     Notes
VCC / +3.3V  1             —        Use 3.3 V, not 5 V. Pin 17 is also
                                    3.3 V if pin 1 is taken.
GND          6             —        Any GND works. 6 is next to SDA/SCL.
SDA          3             GPIO 2   Fixed I2C1 SDA.
SCL (SCLK)   5             GPIO 3   Fixed I2C1 SCL.
SQW          —             unused   Leave open.
32K          —             unused   Leave open.

After the i2c-rtc,ds3231 overlay: RTC is 0x68 (usually shows as UU).
EEPROM on the same module stays at 0x57 for EEPROM.py.

Do not put the DS18B20 data line on pins 3 or 5. Those are the I2C pins.
DS18B20 (1-Wire)INFO_CENTER reads /sys/bus/w1/devices/28-*. That is the
kernel 1-Wire bus, default pin GPIO 4.

DS18B20      Physical pin  BCM      Notes
VDD / +3.3V  17            —        Pin 1 is also 3.3 V. Do not use 5 V.
GND          9             —        Any GND. 9 is near pin 7.
DATA / DQ    7             GPIO 4   Default dtoverlay=w1-gpio pin.

Put a 4.7 kΩ resistor between DATA and +3.3 V.If you ever move DATA off
GPIO 4, you must change the overlay, for example:
    dtoverlay=w1-gpio,gpiopin=17.
The Python code does not select the pin; the kernel does.

WS2812B strip Matches CONFIG.py: strip_gpio = 21.
WS2812B      Physical pin  BCM      Notes
DIN          40            GPIO 21  PCM data out. This is the only Pi
                                    pin that drives the strip.
GND          39            —        Best Pi-side ground: it sits next to
                                    pin 40. Tie this to LED-PSU negative
                                    and strip GND.
+5V          not from the Pi  —

Pins 2 and 4 are the Pi’s 5 V rail. Do not use them for a 16×32 panel.
Feed strip 5 V from the external supply.

Pins 2 and 4 are only listed so you know what they are. For this build
they stay unused by the strip. A 330 Ω resistor goes in the DIN line
near the Pi. A bulk capacitor goes across strip 5 V and GND at the strip
input.

Header picture in words

Top row (odd pins), relevant bits:
Pin 1: 3.3 V      → DS3231 VCC  
Pin 3: GPIO 2 SDA → DS3231 SDA  
Pin 5: GPIO 3 SCL → DS3231 SCL  
Pin 7: GPIO 4     → DS18B20 DATA  
Pin 9: GND        → DS18B20 GND  
Pin 17: 3.3 V     → DS18B20 VDD  
Pin 39: GND       → LED strip GND (common with LED PSU)

Even row:
Pin 2 / 4: Pi 5 V — do not power the panel from these  
Pin 6: GND        → DS3231 GND  
Pin 40: GPIO 21   → WS2812B DIN

Three grounds (DS3231, DS18B20, strip) must all end up on the Pi GND net.
The LED +5 V net stays on the external PSU only.

That changes the documentation tone. For board #6 you are not teaching
jumper-wire GPIO — you are documenting a known HAT pinout plus two 3-pin
jacks.Keep the same electrical map, just describe it as the board
already implements it:
On the HAT (soldered)  DS3231 VCC → header pin 1 (3.3 V)  
DS3231 SDA → pin 3 / GPIO 2  
DS3231 SCL → pin 5 / GPIO 3  
DS3231 GND → pin 6

Temp 3-pin (wired on the PCB to the 1-Wire default)  +3.3 V → pin 17  
DATA → pin 7 / GPIO 4  
GND → pin 9  
4.7 kΩ pull-up on the board between DATA and 3.3 V

LED 3-pin  DIN → pin 40 / GPIO 21  
GND → pin 39, and that ground must also bond to the external LED PSU  
+5 V on that connector should come from the external supply, not header pins 2/4

That last point is the one to label on the silkscreen. People will
assume a 3-pin LED jack is “Pi 5 V / GND / DATA.” If your LED +5 V pin
is tied to pins 2/4, a 16×32 will brown the Pi out. If it is a separate
5 V incoming from the PSU, say so on the board and in the manual.
